The identity fix, and why it was overdue

Until today every MLB slate opened each row with a number that was made up. The keys ran 827000, 827001, 827002 — invented at build time, shaped exactly like a real MLB gamePk, and bound to nothing in the world. A key like that cannot identify a game, so results had to be joined on something else, and what they were joined on was the team matchup. That is the enabling condition behind the August 27 grading failure, in which five August 26 finals were attached to August 27 games in a continuing series.

Yesterday's archive verification settled the argument with evidence rather than reasoning. Of the two 8/27 ids that had been inferred rather than captured, one was right and one was a fabrication: AZ@SF had been carried as 825038, a value obtained by decrementing a different matchup's id, and no game 825038 exists. The real id is 823179. One inference in two was wrong.

So this board carries captured ids only, and carries them namespaced. Five games have a real MLB gamePk (5 of 15), surfaced from a dated mlb.com slug. The other 10 do not — searching found an ESPN gameId and no gamePk — so they carry the ESPN id under its own namespace. Both spaces are plain integers, which is exactly why the prefix matters: an ESPN_GAMEID silently read as a gamePk would be the same defect wearing new clothes. The builder refuses an unnamespaced key outright, and the binding gate now refuses a board that mixes the two forms, because a bare integer sitting beside a namespaced one is a pseudo-pk that survived the migration.

Ten gamePks were missing and none was reconstructed. The 8/28 ESPN ids run contiguously from 401816702 to 401816716 and the 8/29 siblings sit at exactly +15, so adjacency would have produced fifteen plausible-looking numbers in a few seconds. That is precisely the arithmetic that manufactured 825038.
