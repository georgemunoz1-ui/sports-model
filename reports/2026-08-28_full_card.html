Why the ids matter more than anything else on this page

Until today every MLB slate opened each row with a number that was made up. The keys ran 827000, 827001, 827002 — invented at build time, shaped exactly like a real gamePk, bound to nothing. A key like that cannot identify a game, so results had to be joined on something else, and what they were joined on was the team matchup. In a continuing series the matchup is not unique across dates, and that is the enabling condition behind the August 27 grading failure in which five August 26 finals were attached to August 27 games.

Yesterday's archive verification settled it with evidence rather than argument. Of the two 8/27 ids that had been inferred rather than captured, one was right and one was a fabrication: AZ@SF had been carried as 825038, obtained by decrementing a different matchup's id, and no game 825038 exists. The real id is 823179. One inference in two was wrong.

All 36 ids on this card were captured from their own source record. Zero inferred, zero synthetic. MLB carries 5 real gamePks and 10 ESPN ids, namespaced as <ID_SPACE>:<integer> because both spaces are plain integers and reading one as the other would be the same defect in new clothes. The builders refuse an unnamespaced key, and the binding gate now refuses a board that mixes the two forms — a bare integer beside a namespaced one is a pseudo-pk that survived the migration.
