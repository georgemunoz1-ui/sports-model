What v1.0 changes, beyond this page

Acquisition stops being an excuse

The route ladder is seven rungs and a blocked rail advances it. Termination is only legal when every authorized route has been tried, and exhaustion is recorded as an acquisition incident — never as a property of the game. A search snippet may identify an artifact; it may not supply an authoritative field while a stronger route is untried.

Eight checkpoints run from T−24h to the pre-execution quote, with escalation that starts at T−90 rather than at the freeze. By the time the board locks, every automatic remedy has already been tried.

A missing input stops ending a game

A material change now generates a replacement forecast under a preregistered identity with its own hashes, anchored before first pitch, graded separately — the original untouched. The two 8/24 games I blocked for a starter change would now produce replacements instead.

Seven fallback modes are frozen before the slate with distinct identities. A mode invented after seeing the market is refused by name: that is a discretionary adjustment wearing a model’s name.

The vocabulary narrows

Normal final decisions are BET or PASS. Twelve internal conditions — UNCERTAINTY_PIPELINE_DEFECT, PERSONNEL_UNVERIFIED, STARTER_UNCONFIRMED and the rest — are work queues and may not be published as a game’s reason after the freeze. Seven section-11 states can legitimately stop a decision; none of them is “I did not look”.

Prices get a phase

A quote is PRE_GAME, LIVE, HALFTIME, FINAL or UNVERIFIED, and only the first may enter a pregame overlay — refused by the clock and by a declared phase, whichever is stricter. UNVERIFIED is a refusal, not a shrug.

Acceptance — §12 base 12/12 · amendment 1 4/4 · combined 16/16

The standard says it is implemented only when automated tests prove it. Each test asserts both directions — the compliant case passes and the violating case is refused — because a one-directional acceptance test is how a standard becomes decoration.

The remediation, and who owns it

Sections 7 and 10 make this an autonomous maintenance task that runs before the next production board — not something discovered at decision time. In order:

  • Restore provenance on the 1,684-row pool. 1,620 rows carry no evidence path; 1,554 name statsapi with no retained payload. This one is blocked on egress and is the root of the other two.
  • Rebuild the OOF history date-blocked. Restoring the five provisional dates takes admissible clusters from 2 to 7, which clears the floor of 6 by one and is still not adequate: a k=7 estimate has a wide sampling distribution and one extreme cluster moves it.
  • Specify and freeze the estimator: all eleven parts, an identity, hashes, coverage validation actually run, and a stated method for turning probability uncertainty into conservative EV.

Only then does a decision board become a legitimate output. Until then this page is the output, and it is the honest one.

AND THAT 26.22% IS AN APPROXIMATION, NOT A PROBABILITY. It rests on two assumptions that are worth naming rather than burying:

  • Normality. The difference of two binomial proportions is treated as normal. At n=30k/100k with p near 0.5 that is very good — and it is still an approximation.
  • Independence. The ten per-game gaps are treated as ten independent trials. They share a model, a prior and a slate, so the joint law is not known to be a product. If the gaps are positively dependent, the true max-of-ten probability is lower than this figure.
  • Post-hoc maximum. The game was picked for being the largest. That is what the max-of-ten correction exists to handle, and the correction is only as good as the independence assumption underneath it.

A9 · A terminal bin is an event, not a value

Adding an overflow bin fixed the infinite log score and introduced a subtler error, which you caught. A bin holding P(Y ≥ 41) is not an exact-integer pmf. It assigns no probability to 41, or to 42, or to any single integer — and under EXACT_INTEGER_MASS the scorer asks for P(Y = y). Above the support there is no such number to give.

So the outcome space changes, not just the distribution. The choice is between coarsening everything to {0…40, 41+} or spreading the tail mass over an unbounded integer tail with a stated decay. This repository takes the first, because the second adds a parameter no data here has ever fitted or tested. Coarsening applies to every arm and every realised outcome: coarsen_outcome() runs on y before scoring, or a coarsened forecast gets graded against a raw integer it never claimed to resolve.

A10 · What model uncertainty is, and why two blocks cannot supply it reliably

The two-block SD exists but is too unstable for decision use. (This replaces an earlier sentence of mine, “two numbers are not a standard deviation”, which is simply false.) At k=2 the sample SD is perfectly well defined: it equals |r₁ − r₂| ÷ √2. The problem was never existence. With one degree of freedom the 95% interval for the true σ given s spans roughly [0.446 s, 31.9 s] — a factor of about 71 end to end. That is not an error bar, it is an admission. The status is REFUSED_TOO_UNSTABLE_FOR_DECISION_USE, with the computed value shown, and it is a different refusal from k=1, where the ddof=1 SD genuinely is undefined.

The estimand, stated so nothing else can wear the name

Let a date block b hold nb graded forecasts (pi, yi). The block-mean signed calibration residual is

r_b = (1/n_b) Σ_i (y_i − p_i), and σ̂ = sqrt( Σ_b (r_b − r̄)² / (k − 1) ), ddof=1, blocks are dates.

Dates and not games, because forecasts made on one day share a feature vintage, a slate and a model state; treating games as independent draws counts one correlated error many times over. And the residual is signed and averaged within the block — that is what makes the coin flip shrink as 1/√nb while real miscalibration survives.

What may never be substituted for it

LookalikeWhy it is not the estimand
BERNOULLI_OUTCOME_SDsqrt(p(1-p)) — outcome noise, not model error. Does not shrink with data and is largest where the model is best calibrated.
CROSS_GAME_P_SDdispersion of p across a slate — signal strength, not error.
MONTE_CARLO_SDsimulation noise at a trial count — shrinks by running longer, which would buy confidence without evidence.
PRIOR_PARAMETER_SDposterior SD of one arm's prior parameter — not the deployed model's forecast error.
REALISED_EDGE_SDSD of realised betting edge — a downstream P&L statistic that mixes model error with price movement.

The Bernoulli one is the dangerous impostor. sqrt(p(1−p)) is about 50pp at p≈0.5, has the right units, and is computable from the forecast alone with no data at all. It is outcome noise: it does not shrink as evidence accumulates, it is largest exactly where the model is best calibrated, and it says nothing whatever about how wrong p is.
