core breakdown

Everything learned since the project started, the incident that taught it, and the mechanism that now enforces it — or the admission that nothing does.
2026-07-27 · HEAD 91d2d9c · 54 commits (generated at b9257bb/52; re-stamped 2026-07-27) · companion to model_breakdown.html (patch notes)
The one-line version of everything below: this project's actual output has been falsification, not prediction. The pitcher index died. Sides died. The GBM did worse than the thing it replaced. E1 — the "biggest known gap" — came back NO-PROMOTE. The clock finding was withdrawn. The shrinkage convergence turned out to be two different estimators wearing the same number. Each of those is a thing the system now will not do, and the list of things it will not do is the only asset that has compounded. Nine paper plays is not.

1 · The objective, which was wrong at the start

The first and largest correction was not a modeling error. It was that I described the goal as beating the house at a high clip, winning more often than not — and that is not the goal. A bet is judged by expected value against the price's break-even. A 45% dog at +150 is a good bet. A 60% favorite at −200 is a bad one. Getting this wrong doesn't produce a slightly worse model; it produces a model optimized for the wrong thing, which will happily trade away EV to protect a win rate.

This is now AUTH-7, and it is the only place in the project with a retired-language list — phrases I am not permitted to use because using them re-imports the error. It is enforced in code: the selector requires EV > 0, never p > 0.5, and the ledger convention line says so in state.json so a future session reads it before it reads any record. 

RETIRED: "beat the house at a high clip" · "win more often than not"
REQUIRED: "positive EV vs the price's break-even"

2 · The failure class that produced five separate errors

Summaries are not sources. Every data error this project has found — every single one — came from trusting something's description of the data instead of the data. Five incidents, one root cause, and it keeps recurring because the summary is always the convenient artifact.
IncidentWhat the summary saidWhat was true

The last one is the most instructive because it happened this week, in this project, by me, after the doctrine forbidding it was already written down. I inferred a count from a truncated display. That is precisely the WSH error in a different costume. You corrected it with the source, which is the only thing that ever corrects it.

Two doctrines came out of this class and both are load-bearing: "Parse raw locally, never trust the model-reported COUNT/SUM" and "Never grade from a single endpoint." Graded inputs now resolve 2-of-3 across sources.

And the honest gap: WebFetch structurally violates the first doctrine. It pipes content through a summarizing model and hands back prose. Every web-sourced number in this project has passed through exactly the mechanism the doctrine forbids. I have flagged this and I have not solved it, because the environment permits no other fetch path. I will not claim a clean data pipeline until bytes reach disk unaltered.

3 · The lessons, and where each one lives

L1 · from n=1,461 games
The market is the referee, and on public data it wins.
IncidentV13's Monte Carlo simulator, built carefully over weeks, scored a held-out Brier of 0.2500 — exactly a coin flip — against a market at 0.238. Directional 53.0% vs a 52.0% home base rate; the market runs ~57%. A gradient-boosted model on the same features did worse: 0.2563 and 49.7%.
LessonThe GBM result is the important half. Two very different algorithms hitting the same ceiling means the ceiling is the inputs. Season-level rates and team form do not carry what the market prices — lineups, platoons, bullpen state, injuries, order flow.
IncorporatedR3 freezes the verdict. Sides are DEAD; no ML, RL or sides plays. Reopening requires a new feature class beating market Brier on a ≥500-game holdout, not a better algorithm on the same features.
L2 · from the 7/26 board
A price is a structure, not a number.
IncidentAn aggregator board showed best-price-across-books, which silently mixes one book's main line with another's alt rung. It contaminated 10 of 30 arms. Naive ingestion would have produced 11 fake R5-level UNDER edges and a shrinkage fit 65% too aggressive. Per-book diagnostics showed only BetRivers behaving like a main line: mean signed dispersion −0.11 and MAE 0.49, against DK at −1.44 / 1.44.
LessonRung coherence (T2) passed every contaminated row — an internally consistent alt ladder looks fine from inside. Only cross-book dispersion (T3) could see it. A test that cannot fail on the error you have is not protecting you.
IncorporatedFour-test suite with T2 demoted to necessary-but-not-sufficient and T3 load-bearing at ≤1.0 K. Drops declare source_type; on a single-book capture T3 is inapplicable rather than skipped, because one book's page has no alt ladder to confuse.
L3 · from Fedde, Freeland, Avila, Baz
A short posted line is information, not an opportunity.
IncidentFedde came back at EV +0.469 — over the R5 audit threshold, which fired correctly. The audit found the cause: the book posted 2.5 K because it expected an opener or a bulk arm. The engine's E[TBF] prior cannot read a posted line, so it projected a normal start and manufactured a 47% edge out of a workload disagreement.
LessonWhen the market encodes something the model structurally cannot see, the gap is not edge. It is the model being uninformed, expressed in EV units.
IncorporatedHard hold on any arm posted ≤3.5 K, overridable only with an explicit flag. This is a blunt instrument standing in for an opener detector that does not exist yet.
L4 · from a 0.001 EV discrepancy
Look-ahead reintroduces itself through convenience.
IncidentA replay produced EV +0.073 against the ledger's +0.074. Chasing it: the frozen card stores ek: 5.68 while proj() returns 5.680745 — a 2dp rounding difference. But the reason proj() could be called at all is that it reads the pitcher log as it stands now, which on a replay includes the very start being priced.
LessonR2 is not a thing you satisfy once. Any function that reads live state is a leak waiting for a replay. And the discrepancy that looks like harmless rounding is worth chasing — this one was rounding sitting on top of a look-ahead path.
Incorporatedresolve_ek() takes full precision from the live projector only while it still agrees with the frozen card to within 0.005 with matching GS; otherwise it falls back to the card and says out loud that the log has moved.
L5 · from a 7–3 record that was thrown away
A rule you cannot write down in advance is not a rule.
Incidentml_tracking was 7–3 and got frozen anyway. The stated reason: "Selection rule was 'V13 directional lean + my discretion', which is not an EV rule and was never written down in advance."
LessonA winning record produced by an unwritten rule is unfalsifiable and therefore worthless — there is no way to know what would have been excluded. Discarding a 7–3 is cheap; discarding a 70–30 later would not be.
IncorporatedR7. ml_ev_tracking replaced it, pre-registered 2026-07-25T06:20Z with a timestamp before first pitch and mechanical exclusions. When the written rule and the code disagreed (text said "TBD or <5 GS", code said gs<2 or TBD), the resolution was to grade consistently with the code and amend the text forward only — never to re-grade history into agreement.
L6 · from every sample under 30
Small samples get recorded, not obeyed.
IncidentThree live temptations. A raw-vs-shrunk A/B where raw won on both Brier (0.2435 vs 0.2584) and log-loss (0.6806 vs 0.7102) — at n=4. An ML ROI of 46.7% — at n=3. A calibration temperature that said 0.55 at n=26 and 1.50 at n=1,461, both pinned at a boundary.
LessonAll three point somewhere. None of them can carry a verdict. The n=3 ROI is annotated in the ledger itself as "a number, not evidence."
IncorporatedR4, plus the habit of leaving a verdict explicitly UNRESOLVED rather than picking the reading that flatters. calibration_T is still UNRESOLVED for exactly this reason.
L7 · from the 7/25–7/26 shrinkage work · NEW 7/27
Name the estimator and the convention, not just the number.
IncidentI reported two "convergent" measurements that the engine is over-shrunk: 1.35 from outcomes, 1.42 from the market. They are different estimands. The outcome figure is a calibration slope — actual regressed on projected. The market figure is 1/slope of E[K] regressed on line. Since slope(y~x)·slope(x~y) = corr², inverting one does not produce the other.
ProofApply the market estimator to the outcome data: 1/slope(proj~actual) = 1/0.1760 = 5.68. Not 1.35. The convergence was an artifact of using a different estimator on each side.
LessonBeyond the arithmetic: the two conventions optimize different things. Restoring dispersion amplifies my own noise along with any signal, and noise in excess of the market's is precisely what manufactures edges. So the logged KPROP-EDGE-ARTIFACT (residual vs posted line at −0.87) cannot be the readout for this experiment — a 1.42 stretch zeroes that correlation whether or not the extra spread is real.
PendingNext-action #2 is wrong as written. The pre-registration must name estimator, convention and readout metric, and the readout must be held-out MAE and log-loss on plays the selector would actually make.

L8 · found 7/27
A changing selector silently changes what the counter counts.
IncidentThe 7/27 short-line guard blocks any arm posted ≤3.5 K. Buehler (O 3.5) is one of the nine graded plays. Eight of nine would register under today's selector; the ninth would not. Nothing in any ledger row records which selector version produced it.
LessonThe gate is "n ≥ 100 graded plays," which quietly assumes the hundred plays came from one strategy. This is the third selector change. Without a version tag the sample is a blend, and R6 correctly forbids the tempting fix of retro-dropping the row.
PendingProposal: tag every row with selector_version, keep the historical counter at 9 as the audit trail, and define the gate on the current version prospectively — which starts at 0, not 8 and not 9. Eight of the nine survive today's guard, but a filter applied after the fact, with the results already known, is not a selection rule.
L9 · found 7/27
A push is not a win.
Incidentp_over computes P(X ≥ ⌈line⌉). Correct on a half-point line. On an integer line, ⌈5⌉ = 5 yields P(X≥5), which scores X=5 as a win when it is a push — 17.6% of outcomes at E[K]=5, 15.9% at E[K]=5.68 on a 6. ev() has the matching gap: a push returns stake and needs a three-outcome form.
LessonRare on main lines, not rare on alt lines — and the ingester now accepts alt captures, so the exposure grew at the moment intake got lenient.
IncorporatedFixed in commit 91d2d9c. probs(ek,line) is now the single source of truth and ev(p,ml,p_push) settles three outcomes; the default p_push=0.0 reproduces every half-point call bit-for-bit. Self-test 4 → 8 checks. Independently re-verified 2026-07-27.
L10 · from AUTH-6
Not all losses are the same loss.
IncidentA LIVE loss inside a 2-run margin is variance. A blowout loss — ≥5 runs and never competitive — is the model having been wrong about the game, not unlucky in it.
IncorporatedBlowout-loss rate is a primary KPI, baseline 2/13 = 15%, with an 18% guardrail on the card. This is the one component of the sides work that survived validation: picks flagged ≥18% got blown out 33% of the time versus 5% for the rest, a 6.6× separation. The simulator could not pick winners and could still tell you when it was likely to be badly wrong.
L11 · from V13's "80%+ locks"
Displayed confidence is a claim and must be calibrated like one.
IncidentV13's high-confidence picks — the ones displayed as 80%+ — won 61%. Its honest operating range is roughly 40–65%.
IncorporatedCalibration compresses displayed confidence toward the honest range. A number shown to you is a number that can be graded, so it gets graded.
L12 · standing
Price access is the binding constraint, not model quality.
EvidenceEverything downstream of a price is in reasonable shape. What is not: getting a trustworthy price, before first pitch, consistently. No closing price has ever been captured; the close_* columns in results.csv contain opening prices, and the historical closes are gone. That single gap blocks E3 and the favorite-fade E4.
IncorporatedThe only structural response so far is to lower the capture bar rather than raise the capture effort — one arm, one book, both sides is a complete drop, and gate eligibility turns on the clock alone.

6 · The through-line

Two things separate the parts of this project that held up from the parts that didn't, and neither is about modeling.

The first is who gets to be the referee. Everything that survived was measured against something that could not be argued with — outcomes, the market's Brier, a second independent source, a frozen pre-registration. Everything that had to be discarded was validated against my own reasoning: the 7–3 ML record, the shrinkage convergence, the clock finding, the fantasy call. In every one of those I was checking my work against my own description of the data rather than the data.

The second is that the useful moves have been subtractive. The strongest thing built this week was not a feature; it was making the intake demand less and the clock demand more, because the old requirement was mine and not the market's, and it was costing every board its gate eligibility. The same shape shows up in the ≤3.5 hold, the T3 quarantine, the frozen sides verdict, and the retired language list. This system gets better mostly by refusing to do things.

Which is why the record stands where it does and why I will keep stating it plainly: nine graded paper plays, 4–5, −1.61 units, against a gate of one hundred. That is not a track record. It is the first one percent of one, and the four defects found in the last day — three of them found by you reading the document rather than by me writing it — are a fair estimate of how much is still wrong that neither of us has looked at yet.
