01

Scope the three-arm claim

What was wrong

The summary called a board three-arm on the strength of its best sport. Neither a missing arm nor a null arm is visible to a row count.

The rule now

Coverage is computed per sport and per estimand. An arm equal to the baseline on every paired game is BASELINE_EQUIVALENT_NULL. COMPLETE_THREE_ARM is refused while any declared sport is short an arm anywhere.

The test is exact equality, never a tolerance — one differing game is a real challenger, and a tolerance would let me declare a working arm null to tidy a table.

PARTIAL_THREE_ARM_BOARD — mlb winner 3/3, mlb total 2/3, wnba winner 2/3, nfl winner 1/3. arm_coverage.py, 14/14 mutants killed.

02

Fix the totals estimand

What was wrong

Each arm's line was floor(mu)+0.5 from its own mean, and over/under was scored against it. Every arm then scores about 50/50 by construction, and a model projecting every game at 30 runs looks exactly as calibrated as one projecting the truth. The card labelled this honestly; labelling a self-referential statistic does not make it an estimand.

The rule now

Refused. Totals are graded three ways instead: point error against the actual total, carrying MAE with its median, its signed bias and its largest miss together; the full simulated distribution against the actual, by log score and CRPS; and market over/under only on an independently captured pregame line.

All 15 rows refused as REFUSED_SELF_REFERENTIAL_OWN_FAIR_LINE. Log score and CRPS are NOT_COMPUTABLE for this cohort — no arm retained a distribution — and that is reported rather than back-filled from a point projection. totals_estimand.py, 21/21.

03

Add a real total baseline

What was wrong

Worse than absent. The baseline row carried the challenger's total in 15 of 15 games, identical to the third decimal. A baseline whose numbers are the candidate's numbers cannot lose.

A second defect surfaced while checking it: the baseline reports p(home) 0.5252 everywhere while projecting the away team to outscore the home team in six of those games. Two unrelated numbers were wearing one arm's name.

The rule now

The expanding league scoring prior, optionally park-adjusted and adjusted by nothing else. A baseline that knows about the teams is not a baseline.

8.984 runs over 1,802 tier-1 finals before the cutoff, PRIOR_POOL_SD_RUNS 4.537. A prior pool containing a game dated on or after the cutoff raises rather than being filtered quietly.

04

Restore the 100,000-trial standard

What was wrong

The champion runs 30,000 trials and was being described under the current standard.

The rule now

30k is LEGACY_TRIAL_COUNT: still registered, still gradeable, and barred from claiming the standard. A standard raised today does not retroactively unmake yesterday's forecasts.

The 100k arm is a separate identity, ml.v13_1.hfa1005.card.s722n100000 — a fork, never a re-tag, so the two can never be pooled or substituted. Both other arms carry simulation_trials 0 and are closed form, so no trial standard applies to them. simulation_standard.py, 16/16.

05

Common random numbers

What was wrong

Several reported arm deltas sat near one standard deviation of pure seed noise, where re-running the same two models with different seeds could reorder them.

The rule now

The stream is keyed on slate, game and simulation version, and accepts and ignores model_id — so arms share coin flips and keep distinct identities. Registration refuses two arms that share a model identity.

The honest limit: common random numbers change a difference's variance by an unknown covariance, so the independent-seed formula is not a valid bound under them. Delta noise is reported DELTA_NOISE_UNQUANTIFIED unless a paired standard deviation is actually measured across seeds.

One correction to the premise. 0.41pp assumes both arms are simulated. Only the champion is, so the delta inherits 0.289pp, not 0.408pp — and common random numbers cannot apply to a difference with one stream in it. They become load-bearing the moment the 100k fork exists.

The conclusion survives for one game rather than five: only ATL@MIL at +0.50pp (z = 1.71) fails the two-sigma bar. TOR@NYY (z = 2.42) and LAA@TEX (z = 2.18) clear it, narrowly.

06 · 07 · 10

One name per quantity, and a reason that renders whole

What was wrong

“Spread” meant a handicap to every reader and a cross-game standard deviation to the board. Directly beneath it two genuinely different uncertainties were both printed as generic headings. And the pill carrying a blocked action's reason was cut off mid-word.

The rule now

A closed vocabulary carries each quantity's banned aliases with its definition. Every quantity declares a family, and CROSS_GAME_P_SD is dispersion — it says how much an arm separates games, not how unsure it is about one. A label must name the kind it carries, which refuses an abbreviated heading as firmly as a bare one. Every status token must appear verbatim; a token present only as a prefix is a truncation and is refused, not styled.

The 8/23 render fails with 14 problems across all three families. The alias check is scoped to the labels a number is named by — the ban is on the column heading, not on the English word inside a sentence explaining it. render_contract.py, 24/24.

08

One canonical franchise id

What was wrong

The reference wrote WAS@POR and the card WAS@PDX; the same slate spells one MLB game CIN@AZ and CIN@ARI. A key spelled two ways does not fail loudly — it finds no partner and drops the game, which is how ATL came to show 23 games in one view and 97 in another.

Measured in this repository: PDX/POR 24/5, PHX/PHO 32/5, LVA/LV 31/2, NYL/NY 19/2.

The rule now

One canonical id per franchise per sport, applied at the layer where arms are paired rather than inside each model. An unrecognised token is refused, never passed through — the silent passthrough is the whole bug, because a token that survives a join becomes a new franchise.

Both slates were losing exactly one paired game to spelling: 1 in the WNBA, 1 in MLB. The WNBA table is checked at import against the champion model's own alias map, so the two cannot drift apart silently. franchise_identity.py, 15/15.

09

Strengthen the chronology proof

What was wrong

Start time plus seven hours is an inference, and it was sharing a verdict with an observation. The maximum is an assumption, not a measurement: extra innings, weather delays and suspended-and-resumed games all break it, in a direction a timestamp cannot be wrong in.

The rule now

Three grades that never share a status: a tier-1 sourced instant, an unsourced instant, and a derived bound. The bound's assumption and its known failure modes travel with the verdict, and so does its margin — a bound clearing by nineteen hours and one clearing by ninety seconds are not the same claim. A result whose ordering cannot be established is excluded from the prior pool with a reason and counted, never left in quietly.

Under the default policy all 15 rows are admitted, every one of them on a bound, with a smallest margin of 14.83 hours. Under STRICT_OBSERVED_ONLY all 15 are excluded — this repository stores no official finalization timestamps at all. That is the honest state, and it is why the strict policy is offered rather than imposed. finalization_evidence.py, 16/16, wired into prequential.audit_chronology, now 29/29.

What is still open

  • The 8/23 MLB cohort is valid prospective evidence and is not yet settled. Grading runs per sport, arm, estimand and per-game information dose.
  • The real question is the one the owner named: whether a prior-heavy challenger buys calibration at the cost of discrimination. The four flips plus the largest movers will carry most of that signal, and neither Brier skill nor a record answers it alone.
  • Log score and CRPS need a retained simulated distribution. That has to be wired before the next card, not reconstructed after.
  • Binding gate: 500 games across 60 slate dates. Currently 30 games across 2 dates paired.
  • Betting output remains zero without a price overlay, and no captured board for this slate clears the same-book bar in full.
