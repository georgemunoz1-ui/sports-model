What the system actually is

Two tracks, one alive

AUTH-2 splits the project in two. V11 is frozen — a reference spec, not touched. V13 is the cloud-native line, and its two products have diverged sharply in credibility: a Monte Carlo game simulator for sides and totals that has been measured and found to have no edge, and a K-prop projector that beats a naive baseline in backtest and is currently accumulating paper plays toward an authorization gate.

The K-prop projector — the only thing with a live verdict

krate   = (ΣSO + 0.22·80) / (ΣTBF + 80)        # league K-rate prior, weight 80 TBF
E[TBF]  = (ΣTBF + 24.0·3) / (starts + 3)       # league TBF prior, weight 3 starts
E[K]    = krate · E[TBF]
P(over) = 1 − Σ e^−E[K] · E[K]^i / i!  for i < ⌈line⌉      # Poisson tail, closed form
EV(p,ml) = p·profit − (1−p),   profit = 100/|ml| if ml<0 else ml/100

Accumulation is START-ONLY. Relief outings are excluded, because a reliever's TBF distribution says nothing about a starter's workload. Fewer than 5 career starts in the logs and the projector returns nothing at all rather than a guess.

The selector (select_play) requires all of the following at once:

ConditionThresholdWhy
Games started≥ 5Below this the prior dominates and the "projection" is just the league mean
Expected value> 0The objective is EV against the price's break-even, never win%
Edge over break-even≥ 0.04Buffer against the model's own error
Displacement |proj − line|≥ 0.8 KSmaller displacements sit inside the noise floor
Direction agreementside ≡ sign(disp)An OVER must be backed by a projection above the line, not merely a friendly price
Stake weight0.5 if GS < 10Thin sample gets half size

Validation: n = 1,759 historical starts, MAE 1.92 K against a naive last-start baseline of 2.41, with displaced lines resolving 62–70%. That is the whole case for the track, and it is a backtest case.

The sides simulator (V13.1) — measured, and it lost

runs         ~ Poisson(Gamma(shape, mean/shape))

Park factors are derived from our own results in park_factors.json, with a park×contact interaction. Blowout risk P(lose by ≥5) carries an 18% guardrail per AUTH-6. A game is flagged capped when either starter has <2 GS or is TBD.

All of that produced a held-out Brier of 0.2500 against a market at 0.238. The problem is not that the simulator is badly built. It is that a well-built simulator running on public season-level inputs does not beat a market that has those same inputs plus lineups, weather, injury wires and order flow. That is the finding, and it is why sides are frozen rather than iterated on.

The authorization gate

All four must hold. Any one failing means no.
  1. n ≥ 100 graded K-prop plays — currently 9.
  2. ROI > 0 with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval excluding zero — currently −1.61u, so this is not close.
  3. Calibration holds forward, not merely in backtest.
  4. Explicit authorization from Taco. Not implied by the first three.
A nine-play sample is neither a bad result nor a good one. It is not a result. At this size the record cannot distinguish a real edge from a coin, and I will not narrate it as though it can.

Rules the system runs under

R1Never invent a number. Missing data becomes null plus a flag. Every ledger figure traces to a source.
R2No look-ahead. Features are as-of pregame; grading uses finals only.
R3Frozen things stay frozen. The V11 spec; the n=1,461 sides verdict. No sides plays.
R4Small samples don't move verdicts. Daily grades get logged; verdicts change only at gate sizes.
R5A big edge is probably a bug. Any |EV| > 35% or |proj − line| > 1.5 K triggers an input audit before carding.
R6The corrections log is append-only. Errors get logged, never erased.
R7One parameter per experiment, pre-registered in state.json before evaluation.
R8All web data via WebFetch, verbatim-extraction prompts, one retry on 429.

The honest summary

One track out of three has a live verdict, and that verdict is "keep measuring," not "it works." The sides engine was built carefully, measured honestly, and found to be a coin flip against a market that is meaningfully better than one — so it was frozen rather than tuned, which is the correct response and also the unsatisfying one. Totals are worse. The K-prop track is the one with a real backtest behind it (n = 1,759, MAE 1.92 vs naive 2.41), and it has been given nine live paper plays, which is not enough to conclude anything in either direction.

The most useful thing learned this week was not a modeling result. It was that the ingestion path had been demanding far more than the gate actually required, and that demand kept putting every board in mid-slate where it could not count. The fix was to make the intake lenient and the clock strict. The second most useful thing was that two independent measurements — one against outcomes, one against prices — agree the projections are over-shrunk by roughly 1.35–1.42×. That is the most promising open lead, and under R7 it gets pre-registered and frozen before it gets used, not after.

Real money remains at zero and unauthorized. That does not change because a number looks good on a nine-play sample.
